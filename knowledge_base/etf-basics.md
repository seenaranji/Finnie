# ETF Basics

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of investments, such as many stocks or many bonds, that you can buy or sell as a single item on a stock exchange. Buying one share of an ETF gives you a small piece of everything in the basket.

## How ETFs work
- **Trading:** ETFs trade on an exchange throughout the day, just like individual stocks, so the price changes during the day.
- **What they hold:** many ETFs follow an index, which is a list of companies used to measure part of the market. For example, an ETF might hold the companies in a large-company stock index. Some ETFs are actively managed, meaning a manager chooses what to hold.
- **Ticker symbols:** each ETF has a ticker, like a stock.

## Costs to know about
- **Expense ratio:** a yearly fee taken from the fund, stated as a percentage of what you have invested. A 0.10% expense ratio costs about $1 a year per $1,000 invested.
- **Bid-ask spread:** a small gap between the price buyers will pay and sellers will accept. It is a cost every time you trade.
- Your brokerage may also charge fees. These vary by provider.

## Why people use them
Because one ETF can hold hundreds of investments, it is an easy way to spread money across many companies or bonds. This is called diversification.

## Things to remember
An ETF is not automatically safe. If the investments inside fall in value, the ETF falls too. What matters is what is in the basket.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
