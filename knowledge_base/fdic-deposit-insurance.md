# FDIC Deposit Insurance: What It Covers and What It Does Not

This article is based on the FDIC deposit insurance page, checked on 2026-10-31.

## What it is
The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) provides deposit insurance that protects your money if an insured bank fails. Per the page, deposits are "automatically insured to at least $250,000 at each FDIC-insured bank." The page does not explain how the limit applies to different ownership categories such as joint accounts, so use the FDIC's other resources for that.

## What it covers
Money in deposit accounts at an insured bank, including checking accounts, savings accounts, money market deposit accounts and certificates of deposit (CDs). Coverage is automatic when you open one of these accounts.

## What it does not cover
Insurance applies only to deposit accounts. The FDIC does not insure mutual funds, annuities, life insurance policies, stocks and bonds, crypto assets, municipal securities or the contents of safe deposit boxes. Investments can lose value even if you bought them through a bank.

## Checking and getting help
The FDIC's BankFind tool can confirm that a bank is insured. The page warns that scammers can create fake banks or websites, so confirm an institution is legitimate before sharing personal information or sending money. The FDIC can be reached at 1-877-275-3342 with questions about coverage.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
