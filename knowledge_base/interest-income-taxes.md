# Interest Income: How It Is Taxed

This article summarizes IRS Topic No. 403, last updated 2026-10-07, and checked on 2026-10-31. The page does not name a tax year for its figures, so check current IRS guidance for the year you are filing.

## What counts as interest
Most interest you receive, or that is credited to an account you can withdraw from without penalty, is taxable income "in the year it becomes available to you." Examples include interest from bank accounts, money market accounts and certificates of deposit, corporate bond interest, and interest on Treasury bills, notes and bonds. Treasury interest is taxable federally but exempt from state and local income tax.

Savings bond interest can be reported each year, though for Series EE and Series I bonds you generally wait until maturity or redemption.

## Interest that may not be taxable
- Interest on certain state, District of Columbia or U.S. territory bonds is reportable but not federally taxable.
- Some Series EE and Series I bond interest may be excluded when used for qualified higher education expenses, if the requirements are met.

## Reporting
- **Form 1099-INT:** you should receive one if you were paid interest of $10 or more. Business-paid interest is reported on the form if it is $600 or more.
- **Form 1099-OID:** issued for taxable original issue discount of $10 or more.
- You must report all taxable and tax-exempt interest on your return, even if you did not receive a Form 1099.

## Original issue discount
Part of the discount on a bond or similar debt can be taxed as interest each year, even if you receive no payment that year.
