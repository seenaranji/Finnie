# Treasury Notes

Treasury notes are U.S. government securities with medium-term lengths. This article is based on the TreasuryDirect page checked on 2026-10-31.

## Key facts
- **Terms:** 2, 3, 5, 7 or 10 years.
- **Interest:** a fixed interest payment every six months until the note matures. The rate is set at auction and stays the same for the life of the note. TreasuryDirect says it is "never less than 0.125%."
- **Buying:** the minimum is $100, in $100 steps. The maximum is $10 million for a non-competitive bid or 35% of the offering amount for a competitive bid. Notes are issued in electronic form only.
- **Auctions:** the 2-, 3-, 5- and 7-year notes are auctioned monthly. The 10-year notes are auctioned in February, May, August and November and reopened eight times a year.
- **Holding or selling:** you can keep a note until it matures or sell it earlier. Notes can be converted into STRIPS.

## Taxes
Federal income tax is due each year on the interest earned. There are no state or local taxes on the interest.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
