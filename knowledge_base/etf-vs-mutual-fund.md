# ETFs vs. Mutual Funds

ETFs and mutual funds are alike in an important way: both pool money to hold many investments at once, which gives you diversification. They differ mainly in how they are bought, priced, and often how they are managed.

| | ETF | Mutual fund |
|---|---|---|
| How you trade it | On an exchange, like a stock | Directly with the fund company |
| When the price updates | Continuously during the trading day | Once per day, after markets close |
| Price you get | The market price when you trade | That day's NAV (net asset value) |
| Minimum to start | Often the price of one share | Sometimes a set minimum amount |
| Typical management | Often tracks an index | Either index-tracking or actively managed |
| Main costs | Expense ratio, bid-ask spread | Expense ratio, and sometimes sales loads |

## Careful with generalizations
Many ETFs have low fees and many mutual funds cost more, but this is not a rule. There are low-cost index mutual funds, and there are actively managed ETFs. The fund itself matters more than whether it is an ETF or a mutual fund.

## What to compare for any specific fund
- The expense ratio and any sales charges
- What the fund holds and how it picks its investments
- How closely an index fund follows its index
- Any minimum investment or trading restrictions

## Which is better?
Neither is better in every situation. The choice often depends on how someone wants to buy, how much they invest, and what their retirement plan or brokerage offers.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
