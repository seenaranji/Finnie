# Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

Treasury bills, often called T-bills, are short-term debt securities from the U.S. Department of the Treasury. This article is based on the TreasuryDirect page checked on 2026-10-31.

## How they work
- **Terms:** bills mature in 4, 6, 8, 13, 17, 26 or 52 weeks. (Cash Management Bills are sold at irregular times and with variable terms, only through a bank, broker or dealer, not through TreasuryDirect.)
- **How you are paid:** a bill is sold at a discount or at par, and "when the bill matures, you are paid its face value." The interest is the difference between what you paid and the face value. It is paid at maturity, and the rate is fixed at auction.
- **Buying:** the minimum is $100, in $100 steps. The maximum is $10 million for a non-competitive bid and 35% of the offering amount for a competitive bid. Bills are issued in electronic form only.
- **Auctions:** 52-week bills are auctioned every four weeks and the 4-week to 26-week bills are auctioned weekly.
- **Selling early:** you can hold a bill until it matures or sell it before then. Bills are not eligible for STRIPS.

## Taxes
Interest is subject to federal income tax but exempt from state and local taxes.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
