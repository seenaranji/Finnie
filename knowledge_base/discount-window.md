# The Discount Window and Discount Rate

This article is based on a Federal Reserve page last updated May 20, 2024, and checked on 2026-10-31.

## The discount window
The discount window "allows depository institutions and U.S. branches and agencies of foreign banks to borrow from Federal Reserve Banks." Borrowers must first sign legal agreements and pledge collateral, and all loans must be collateralized.

## The discount rate
The page describes the rate for each lending program:
- **Primary credit:** since March 2020, the rate has been set at the top of the FOMC's target range for the federal funds rate. It is available to institutions in generally sound financial condition.
- **Secondary credit:** a higher rate than primary credit, for institutions that do not qualify for primary credit.
- **Seasonal credit:** a rate based on an average of selected market rates, for small institutions with seasonal swings.

Each Reserve Bank's board of directors sets these rates, subject to review by the Board of Governors.

## What it is used for
- **Liquidity:** borrowing helps institutions manage liquidity risk and avoid pulling credit from customers during market stress, which supports the flow of credit to households and businesses.
- **Monetary policy:** if market rates rise above the primary credit rate, banks are encouraged to borrow at that rate, which helps the discount window act as a policy tool.
- **Loan length:** primary credit loans can run overnight or up to 90 days, and borrowers can repay early without penalty.
