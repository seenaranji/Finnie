# Beginner Finance Glossary

Short, plain-language definitions written for Finnie. For official definitions, see the source link.

**401(k):** a retirement plan offered through an employer, funded by paycheck contributions.

**Asset:** anything of value you own, such as cash, stocks, bonds, or property.

**Asset allocation:** how your money is divided among types of assets, such as stocks, bonds, and cash.

**Bid-ask spread:** the small gap between the price buyers offer and the price sellers ask. A cost of trading.

**Bond:** a loan you make to a government or company in return for interest and repayment on a set date.

**Capital gain:** the profit when you sell an investment for more than you paid.

**Compound interest:** interest earned on both your original money and on interest already added.

**Coupon:** the regular interest payment a bond makes, usually quoted as a yearly percentage of face value.

**Diversification:** spreading money across different investments to reduce the impact of any single one doing badly.

**Dividend:** a payment some companies make to shareholders out of profits.

**ETF (exchange-traded fund):** a basket of investments that trades on an exchange like a stock.

**Expense ratio:** the yearly fee a fund charges, as a percentage of the amount invested.

**Face value (par):** the amount a bond repays at maturity.

**Index:** a list of investments used to measure part of the market.

**Index fund:** a fund designed to follow an index instead of trying to beat it.

**Inflation:** a general rise in prices over time, which reduces what money can buy.

**IRA (individual retirement account):** a retirement account you open yourself.

**Maturity date:** the date a bond's face value is repaid.

**Mutual fund:** a pool of investors' money used to buy a collection of investments, priced once a day.

**NAV (net asset value):** the per-share value of a fund, calculated after markets close.

**Portfolio:** all the investments a person holds together.

**Principal:** the original amount of money invested or borrowed.

**Risk tolerance:** how much ups and downs in value a person is comfortable with.

**Roth IRA / Roth 401(k):** retirement accounts funded with after-tax money, where qualified withdrawals are generally tax-free.

**Stock (share):** a small piece of ownership in a company.

**Ticker symbol:** a short code that identifies a stock or fund, such as AAPL.

**Time horizon:** how long until you need the money.

**Traditional IRA / 401(k):** retirement accounts funded with pre-tax money in many cases, where withdrawals are generally taxed as income.

**Volatility:** how much and how fast an investment's price moves up and down.

**Yield:** the income an investment earns, expressed as a percentage of its price.
