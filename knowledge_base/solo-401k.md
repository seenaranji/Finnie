# One-Participant (Solo) 401(k) Plans

A one-participant 401(k), also called a Solo 401(k), is a 401(k) for a business owner with no employees, or for that owner and a spouse. The IRS says it "isn't a new type of 401(k) plan" but a traditional one, so the same rules apply. This article summarizes the IRS page last updated April 9, 2026, and checked on 2026-10-31.

## Two roles, two kinds of contributions
The owner acts as both employee and employer.
- **Elective deferrals (as employee):** money the owner chooses to set aside, up to 100% of compensation and subject to the yearly deferral limit. The page does not state the dollar limit and links to a separate IRS page.
- **Employer nonelective contributions (as employer):** generally up to 25% of compensation as the plan defines it.
- Total contributions, not counting catch-up contributions, cannot exceed the overall annual limit.

## For self-employed owners
"Compensation" means earned income: net self-employment earnings after deducting half of self-employment tax and the contributions made for yourself. Because of this, the owner must do a special calculation. IRS Publication 560 has the rate tables and worksheets.

## Testing and filing
- A business with no common-law employees generally does not need nondiscrimination testing. That changes if it hires employees who meet the plan's eligibility rules.
- A one-participant plan generally must file Form 5500-EZ once its assets reach $250,000 or more at year-end.
