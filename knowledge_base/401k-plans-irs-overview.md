# 401(k) Plans (IRS Overview)

The IRS describes a 401(k) as "a feature of a qualified profit-sharing plan." It lets employees contribute part of their wages to individual accounts. This article summarizes the IRS page checked on 2026-10-31, which is a short overview.

## Key points
- **Traditional 401(k):** the salary you choose to defer is excluded from your taxable income now. Distributions, including earnings, are taxable at retirement.
- **Designated Roth accounts:** a 401(k) plan can offer a Roth option (a "designated Roth account"). The IRS notes that qualified distributions from a designated Roth account are not included in taxable income.
- **Employer money:** employers can also contribute to employees' accounts.
- **Limits:** this IRS page gives no contribution limits and links to a separate page for them. The yearly limits are in the article on contribution limits.

## Where to read more
The IRS links to its 401(k) resource guide and to Publication 4531, a checklist for 401(k) plan sponsors.
