# Traditional IRAs (IRS Summary)

A traditional IRA is, in the IRS's words, "a way to save for retirement that gives you tax advantages." This article summarizes the IRS page checked on 2026-10-31.

## Key points
- **Contributions may be deductible:** money you put in may be "fully or partially deductible, depending on your filing status and income." The income ranges for the current year are in the article on contribution limits.
- **Growth is not taxed each year:** savings in the account, including earnings and gains, generally are not taxed while they stay in the IRA.
- **Taxes come later:** taxes generally apply when you take a distribution (a withdrawal) from the account.
- **Details live elsewhere:** the IRS page links to Publication 590-B for the rules on distributions, and to a separate page for the contribution limits. The page itself contains no dollar amounts.

## Compared with a Roth IRA
With a traditional IRA the tax benefit typically comes when you contribute (a possible deduction) and taxes come when you withdraw. With a Roth IRA there is no deduction when you contribute and qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

Taking money out before age 59½ can bring an additional tax; see the article on the early withdrawal tax.
