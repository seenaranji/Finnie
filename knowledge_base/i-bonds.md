# Series I Savings Bonds (I Bonds)

A Series I savings bond is a U.S. Treasury savings bond whose interest rate changes every six months based on inflation. TreasuryDirect warns: "The rate can go down." This article is based on its page checked on 2026-10-31. It does not list the current rate, because the rate changes; see TreasuryDirect for it.

## How the rate works
The overall rate combines a **fixed rate**, which never changes for that bond, and an **inflation rate**, which resets every six months. Interest is earned monthly and compounds every six months, so the bond grows from both interest and a larger principal.

## Key rules
- **Buying:** as of January 1, 2025, I bonds are only available electronically through a TreasuryDirect account. The minimum is $25. One Social Security Number or Employer Identification Number may buy up to $10,000 in electronic I bonds per calendar year.
- **Holding period:** the bond earns interest for up to 30 years.
- **Cashing early:** you can cash it after 12 months. If you cash it before 5 years, you lose the last 3 months of interest.
- **Getting paid:** interest is paid when you cash the bond, or automatically at maturity for electronic bonds.

## Taxes
Federal income tax applies, but state and local income tax does not. You can choose to report the earnings each year or when you cash the bond. Earnings may be tax-free if used for qualified higher education expenses.

## Things to know
The rate can rise or fall every six months, you receive no money until you cash the bond, and cashing within five years costs three months of interest.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
