# The 10% Additional Tax on Early IRA Withdrawals

Taking money out of an IRA early can cost more than ordinary income tax. This article summarizes IRS Topic No. 557, last updated 2026-10-26, and checked on 2026-10-31.

## The rule
The IRS applies a "10% additional tax" to early withdrawals from traditional and Roth IRAs. Early generally means before the account owner reaches age 59½. The 10% applies only to the part of the withdrawal that is included in gross income, and it is charged on top of regular income tax.

## Exceptions listed by the IRS
The tax does not apply to rollovers or transfers to another IRA or qualified plan (if the one-IRA-to-IRA-rollover-per-year rule is followed). The IRS also lists exceptions, including distributions that are:
- not more than your unreimbursed medical expenses above a certain percentage of your adjusted gross income;
- made because you are totally and permanently disabled, or terminally ill;
- made to a beneficiary or estate after the owner's death;
- part of a series of substantially equal periodic payments;
- not more than your qualified higher education expenses;
- not more than $10,000 used for a qualified first-time home purchase;
- a qualified birth or adoption distribution (up to $5,000);
- made to a victim of domestic abuse, to a person affected by a qualified disaster, or for personal or family emergency expenses, as the IRS describes;
- a corrective distribution, such as a returned contribution withdrawn by the filing due date.

The IRS page has the full list and the conditions for each, so read it before relying on an exception.
