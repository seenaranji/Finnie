# Retirement Plan Types for Self-Employed People

Self-employed people can save for retirement on a tax-deferred basis through several kinds of plans. This article lists them as described on the IRS page checked on 2026-10-31. The dollar limits on that page were for tax year 2024 and limits change each year, so this article does not repeat them; check the current IRS figures.

## The plan types
1. **SEP (Simplified Employee Pension):** set up with a short form and a SEP-IRA at a bank or other financial institution. Contributions can be a percentage of net earnings from self-employment, up to a yearly cap.
2. **401(k) plan:** allows salary deferrals (pre-tax or designated Roth) plus an employer-type contribution based on self-employment earnings. A one-participant version is often called a "solo 401(k)" and avoids discrimination testing when the only employee is the owner's spouse.
3. **SIMPLE IRA plan:** allows employee-type contributions plus either a fixed 2% or a matching 3% employer contribution. It can be set up from January 1 through October 1.
4. **Profit-sharing plan:** the business decides each year how much to contribute, up to a percentage of compensation.
5. **Money purchase plan:** requires a fixed percentage of income each year, based on the plan's formula.
6. **Defined benefit plan:** a traditional pension that pays a stated annual benefit at retirement. An actuary calculates the contributions.

## A note on names
The IRS notes that "Keogh plans" is an older name for these plans and is seldom used today.
