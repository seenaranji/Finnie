# Roth IRAs (IRS Summary)

A Roth IRA is an individual retirement account that, except for the differences below, follows the same rules as a traditional IRA. This article summarizes the IRS page checked on 2026-10-31.

## Key points
- **No deduction going in:** contributions to a Roth IRA are not tax-deductible.
- **Tax-free coming out:** "qualified distributions" are tax-free if you meet the requirements. The IRS points to Publication 590-B for those requirements.
- **Set up as a Roth:** the account must be designated as a Roth IRA when it is set up.
- **One shared limit:** your Roth and traditional IRA contributions share one combined limit. The same combined contribution limit applies across all of your Roth and traditional IRAs.
- **Income can limit contributions:** how much you may contribute can depend on your filing status and modified adjusted gross income (AGI). The current yearly limit and income ranges are in the article on contribution limits.
- **No forced end:** the IRS page says you can leave amounts in the account for as long as you live.

## Where to read more
The IRS directs readers to Publication 590-A (contributions) and Publication 590-B (distributions) for the full rules on setup, contributions and withdrawals.
