# Credit Scores: The Basics

This article is based on a Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) page, checked on 2026-10-31.

## What a credit score is
A credit score estimates how likely a person is to repay a loan on time. It is calculated from information in your credit reports using a mathematical formula called a scoring model.

## What can affect a score
Scoring models typically consider:
- your bill-payment history
- how much unpaid debt you currently have
- the number and type of loan accounts you have
- how long your accounts have been open
- how much of your available credit you are using
- new applications for credit
- debt sent to collection, foreclosure or bankruptcy, and how long ago it happened

## Who uses scores
Companies use credit scores to decide whether to offer mortgages, credit cards, auto loans and other credit, and to set interest rates and credit limits. Scores are also used for tenant screening and insurance decisions.

## There is no single score
A score depends on the data used, the scoring model, the data source and the date it was calculated. The CFPB says: "Most credit scores range from 300-850." A higher score generally makes it easier to qualify for loans and may lead to better rates or terms.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
