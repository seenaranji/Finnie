# Roth vs. Pre-Tax: IRS Comparison Chart

The IRS publishes a chart comparing three account types: the designated Roth 401(k), the Roth IRA and the pre-tax 401(k). This article summarizes the chart's rules as checked on 2026-10-31. The chart lists dollar figures year by year (older years), and one of its required-distribution statements is out of date, so this article leaves those out; use current IRS guidance for amounts and ages.

## Contributions
- **Designated Roth 401(k):** made with after-tax dollars.
- **Roth IRA:** made with after-tax dollars.
- **Pre-tax 401(k):** made with before-tax dollars.

## Income limits to participate
- Designated Roth 401(k): no income limit.
- Pre-tax 401(k): no income limit.
- Roth IRA: income limits apply, based on modified AGI and filing status.

## Withdrawals
- **Roth accounts:** a withdrawal is a "qualified distribution" if the account has been held at least 5 years and the withdrawal is made on account of disability, on or after death, or on or after age 59½. Qualified distributions are not taxed. A Roth IRA qualified distribution can also be used for a first-time home purchase.
- **Pre-tax 401(k):** withdrawals are subject to federal income tax and most state income taxes.

## Required distributions
The IRS chart says a Roth IRA has no requirement to take distributions while the owner is alive. The rules for 401(k) plans have changed over time, so check current IRS guidance for the age and the rules that apply to you.
