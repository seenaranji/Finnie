# Four Warning Signs of a Scam

This article is based on a Federal Trade Commission (FTC) consumer page, checked on 2026-10-31. The page is about scams in general and does not discuss investment scams specifically.

## Four warning signs
1. **Pretending to be someone you know:** scammers may claim to be from the government, a utility, a tech company or a charity. "They use technology to change the phone number that appears on your caller ID," so caller ID cannot confirm who is calling.
2. **Claiming a problem or a prize:** they may say you owe money, have a virus, or won a lottery but must pay a fee to collect.
3. **Pressuring you to act fast:** "Scammers want you to act before you have time to think."
4. **Insisting on a specific payment method:** watch for demands for cryptocurrency, wire transfers, payment apps or gift cards. Some send a check and ask you to deposit it and send money back.

## How to protect yourself
- Do not share personal or financial information in reply to an unexpected request.
- If a message seems to come from a company you use, contact the company through a website or phone number you already trust, not through links or numbers in the message.
- Take your time. "Honest businesses will give you time to make a decision."
- Never pay with cryptocurrency, a wire service, a payment app or a gift card because someone insists.
- Talk to someone you trust before acting.

## Reporting
If you were scammed or saw a scam, report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
