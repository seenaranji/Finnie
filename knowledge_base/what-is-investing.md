# What Is Investing?

Investing means putting money into something, such as a company, a loan to a government, or a fund, with the hope that it will grow in value over time. The catch is that it can also lose value. Nobody can promise you a profit.

## Saving vs. investing
Saving keeps your money safe and easy to reach, for example in a savings account. It grows slowly, but the amount you put in is usually protected. Investing aims for higher growth, but the price of that possibility is risk: your investment can go down, and you might get back less than you put in.

## Why people invest
Prices tend to rise over the years (this is called inflation), so money sitting in cash buys a little less each year. Investing is one way people try to grow their money faster than prices rise. Over long periods, growth can also build on itself, which is called compounding.

## Common things people invest in
Stocks (small pieces of companies), bonds (loans), and funds that hold many of these together, such as ETFs and mutual funds.

## Two ideas to remember
- **Time horizon:** how long until you need the money. Money needed in a few months is usually treated very differently from money not needed for decades.
- **Risk and return:** higher possible gains usually come with a higher chance of loss.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
