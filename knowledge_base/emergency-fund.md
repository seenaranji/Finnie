# Building an Emergency Fund

This article is based on a Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) guide, checked on 2026-10-31.

## What it is
An emergency fund is a cash reserve "specifically set aside for unplanned expenses or financial emergencies," such as car repairs, medical bills or lost income.

## Why have one
- **Protection from a financial shock:** even a minor shock can set you back, and if it becomes debt it can have a lasting effect.
- **Less reliance on credit:** a reserve helps you avoid depending on credit cards or loans when something unexpected happens.
- **Faster recovery:** setting money aside lets you "recover quicker" and get back on track toward your larger savings goals.

## How much?
The sections of the CFPB guide used here give no dollar amounts or time frames, so this article does not either. How much to keep depends on your own expenses and situation.

## Why it comes before investing
Money you may need soon is usually kept somewhere stable and easy to reach, because investments can fall in value just when you need the cash (see the articles on risk and return).

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
