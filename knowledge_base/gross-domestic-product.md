# Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

This article is based on a Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) learning page, checked on 2026-10-31.

## What GDP is
"The value of the final goods and services produced in the United States is the gross domestic product." GDP is a central part of BEA's National Income and Product Accounts, which track the size and makeup of national output and how the income it produces is spent.

## Nominal vs. real
- **Nominal GDP** (also called "current-dollar") is measured at the prices of the period being measured.
- **Real GDP** (also called "chained") has been adjusted to remove the effects of inflation, so different periods can be compared.

When people quote GDP growth as a percentage, they usually mean the change in real GDP from the previous quarter or year.

## How often it is reported
BEA estimates GDP annually and quarterly. For each quarter it publishes three estimates: an advance estimate about a month after the quarter ends, then a second and third estimate that add data that was not available earlier. Quarterly figures are reported at annual rates unless stated otherwise.

## Seasonal adjustment
Seasonal adjustment removes regular yearly patterns, such as weather, holidays and factory schedules, so the remaining changes better reflect underlying activity.

The page does not discuss what GDP leaves out.
