# Retirement Account Contribution Limits (Tax Year 2026)

These figures are for **tax year 2026**, as announced by the IRS in news release IR-2025-111 on 2025-11-13, and were checked against that release on 2026-10-31. The IRS updates these limits regularly, so always confirm the current numbers on irs.gov before acting. This article covers tax year 2026 only.

## Contribution limits for 2026
| Account | 2026 limit | 2025 limit |
|---|---|---|
| 401(k) employee contribution | $24,500 | $23,500 |
| 401(k) catch-up (age 50 and over) | $8,000 | $7,500 |
| 401(k) higher catch-up (ages 60 to 63) | $11,250 (instead of the $8,000) | not stated in the release |
| IRA (traditional and Roth combined) | $7,500 | $7,000 |
| IRA catch-up (age 50 and over) | $1,100 | $1,000 |

A "catch-up" contribution is an extra amount that people age 50 and over are allowed to add on top of the regular limit.

## Roth IRA: income ranges for 2026
Whether you can contribute directly to a Roth IRA depends on your income. The IRS describes a range with a lower and an upper number. **Below the range**, your income does not reduce the amount you may contribute. **Inside the range**, the amount you may contribute is reduced. **Above the range**, you generally cannot contribute directly.
- Single and head of household: between $153,000 and $168,000 (2025: $150,000 to $165,000)
- Married filing jointly: between $242,000 and $252,000 (2025: $236,000 to $246,000)
- Married filing separately: between $0 and $10,000 (unchanged)

## Traditional IRA: deduction ranges for 2026
Anyone with earned income can generally contribute to a traditional IRA, but whether the contribution is **tax-deductible** can depend on income when you (or your spouse) are covered by a workplace retirement plan. The IRS ranges for 2026 are:
- Single and covered by a workplace plan: between $81,000 and $91,000 (2025: $79,000 to $89,000)
- Married filing jointly, the contributing spouse covered by a workplace plan: between $129,000 and $149,000 (2025: $126,000 to $146,000)
- Married filing jointly, the contributing spouse not covered but the other spouse is: between $242,000 and $252,000 (2025: $236,000 to $246,000)
- Married filing separately, covered by a workplace plan: between $0 and $10,000 (unchanged)

## Why this article gives a date
Limits and income ranges change from year to year, which is why this article names the tax year and the source. If you are reading this after 2026, the numbers may be out of date.

Which account fits a person depends on income, taxes, and goals. A licensed tax professional can advise on that. This article is general education, not tax or investment advice.
