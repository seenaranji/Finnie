# Treasury STRIPS

STRIPS stands for Separate Trading of Registered Interest and Principal of Securities. This article is based on the TreasuryDirect page checked on 2026-10-31.

## What they are
STRIPS split a Treasury security into separate pieces. TreasuryDirect says: "The idea of STRIPS is that the principal and each interest payment become separate securities that are treated individually." Each piece is a zero-coupon security that matures separately and has only one payment.

Example from the page: a bond with 10 years left and twenty semiannual interest payments would become 21 separate securities, one for the principal and one for each interest payment.

## Key facts
- **Eligible securities:** notes, bonds and TIPS can be stripped. Bills and floating rate notes cannot.
- **Minimum:** a face amount of $100, and larger amounts must be multiples of $100.
- **Reassembly:** a stripped security can be put back together if the institution, broker or dealer holds all the remaining pieces.
- **Where they trade:** STRIPS are bought, held, sold and redeemed only through a financial institution, broker or dealer, not through TreasuryDirect.

## Taxes
Interest on STRIPS must be reported in the year it is earned, and the institution holding them sends a Form 1099 each year.
