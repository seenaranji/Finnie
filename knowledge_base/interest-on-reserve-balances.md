# Interest on Reserve Balances (IORB)

This article is based on a Federal Reserve page, checked on 2026-10-31.

## What it is
Federal Reserve Banks pay interest on balances that eligible institutions hold in master accounts at the Reserve Banks. The rate is called the IORB rate and is set by the Board of Governors. The Fed calls it "an important tool for the Federal Reserve's conduct of monetary policy."

## How it fits the bigger picture
The page says the FOMC's implementation note "provides the operational settings for the policy tools that support the FOMC's target range for the federal funds rate." The page does not explain the mechanism in detail.

## Other facts from the page
- The payment rules are in Regulation D (12 CFR Part 204).
- The authority to pay interest took effect October 1, 2008, earlier than the original 2011 date, because of the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008.
- The current IORB rate is published each business day at 4:30 p.m. Eastern Time, along with the next business day's rate.
