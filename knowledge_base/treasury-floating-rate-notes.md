# Treasury Floating Rate Notes (FRNs)

Floating rate notes (FRNs) are U.S. Treasury securities whose interest rate changes over time. TreasuryDirect describes them as "relatively short-term investments." This article is based on its page checked on 2026-10-31.

## Key facts
- **Term:** FRNs mature in two years. You can hold one until it matures or sell it earlier.
- **Interest payments:** every three months until maturity.
- **How the rate is built:** the rate is the sum of two parts.
  - The **index rate** follows the highest accepted discount rate of the most recent 13-week Treasury bill, so it resets weekly.
  - The **spread** is set at the note's first auction and stays fixed for the life of the note.
  Interest accrues daily on the par value.
- **Buying:** the minimum is $100, in $100 steps. The maximum is $10 million for a non-competitive bid or 35% of the offering amount for a competitive bid. FRNs are issued in electronic form only and are not eligible for STRIPS.

## Taxes
Interest is subject to federal income tax each year. There are no state or local taxes.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
