# Mutual Funds Basics

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and uses it to buy a collection of investments, such as stocks, bonds, or both. Each investor owns shares of the fund, which represent a slice of everything it holds.

## How mutual funds work
- **Pricing:** a mutual fund is priced once per day, after the markets close. This price is called the net asset value (NAV): the total value of what the fund holds, minus its costs, divided by the number of fund shares.
- **Buying and selling:** you usually buy from, and sell back to, the fund company itself at that day's NAV, rather than trading with other investors during the day.
- **Management style:** some funds are actively managed, where a manager picks investments to try to beat the market. Index funds simply follow a market index.

## Costs to know about
- **Expense ratio:** a yearly fee, shown as a percentage of your investment. Actively managed funds often charge more than index funds.
- **Sales charges (loads):** some funds charge a fee when you buy or sell. Many funds charge none.
- **Minimum investment:** some funds require a minimum amount to get started.

## Why people use them
Like ETFs, they let you hold many investments through one purchase, which spreads risk. Many retirement plans offer mutual funds.

## Things to remember
Fund performance is not guaranteed, and a fund's past results do not predict its future results. Always check the fees and what the fund actually holds.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
