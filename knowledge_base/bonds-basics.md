# Bonds Basics

A bond is a loan. When you buy a bond, you lend money to a government or a company (the issuer). In return, the issuer promises to pay you interest and to return your original amount on a set date.

## Key terms
- **Face value (par):** the amount you get back at the end, often $1,000 per bond.
- **Coupon:** the interest the bond pays, usually stated as a percentage of the face value each year.
- **Maturity date:** the date the issuer repays the face value.
- **Yield:** the return you earn from a bond given the price you pay. It can differ from the coupon if you buy at a price above or below face value.

## Who issues bonds
Governments (such as the U.S. Treasury), local governments (municipal bonds), and companies (corporate bonds). Different issuers carry different levels of risk.

## Main risks
- **Interest rate risk:** when market interest rates rise, existing bonds with lower rates become less attractive, so their prices tend to fall (and the reverse is also true). This matters if you sell before maturity.
- **Credit risk:** the issuer might fail to make payments. This is higher for weaker issuers.
- **Inflation risk:** fixed payments buy less if prices rise quickly.

Bonds are often less volatile than stocks, but they are not risk-free.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
