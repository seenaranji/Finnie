# U.S. Treasury Securities: The Main Types

The U.S. Department of the Treasury borrows money by selling securities. This article describes the main types as listed by TreasuryDirect on 2026-10-31. Terms and rules can change, so check the official site for the current details.

## The five types
| Type | Term | How it pays |
|---|---|---|
| Treasury bills | 4 weeks up to 52 weeks | Sold at a discount from face value; you are paid face value at maturity |
| Treasury notes | 2, 3, 5, 7 and 10 years | Interest is paid every six months |
| Treasury bonds | 20 or 30 years (originally 30 years) | Interest is paid every six months |
| TIPS (inflation-protected) | 5, 10 and 30 years | Interest is paid twice a year; the principal adjusts with the Consumer Price Index |
| Floating rate notes (FRNs) | 2 years | Interest is paid every three months; the rate moves with 13-week Treasury bill rates |

## The idea in one sentence
You lend money to the U.S. government and it pays you back with interest. Longer terms mean your money is tied up longer.

Each type has its own article in this knowledge base with more detail.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
