# SEP IRAs (Simplified Employee Pension)

A SEP is a retirement arrangement in which an employer contributes money to traditional IRAs, called SEP-IRAs, set up for its employees. According to the IRS, a business "of any size, even self-employed," can set one up. This article summarizes the IRS page checked on 2026-10-31.

## Who contributes
Only the employer contributes. Employees cannot add their own money to a SEP. The employer can contribute "up to 25 percent of each employee's pay," and must contribute at the same rate for all eligible employees.

## Who is eligible
Employees who are at least 21, have worked for the employer in at least 3 of the last 5 years, and earn at least the plan's minimum compensation amount.

## Why a business might choose it
Employers whose income varies from year to year can contribute more in good years and less in down years.

## How it works
1. The employer signs a written agreement (for example IRS Form 5305-SEP, a provider's prototype plan, or a custom plan).
2. The employer tells eligible employees about the plan.
3. A SEP-IRA is opened for each eligible employee at a bank, insurer or other financial institution, and the employee chooses the investments.

## Other features
- Employees are always 100 percent vested, so the money is theirs immediately.
- Employers generally have no IRS filing requirement.
- Withdrawals are taxable, and withdrawals before age 59½ generally face a 10 percent additional tax.
- Participant loans are not allowed.
