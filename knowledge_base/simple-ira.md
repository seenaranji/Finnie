# SIMPLE IRA Plans

SIMPLE stands for Savings Incentive Match Plan for Employees. A SIMPLE IRA lets both employers and employees put money into traditional IRAs set up for employees. The IRS calls it "ideally suited as a start-up retirement savings plan for small employers." This article summarizes the IRS page last reviewed April 2, 2026, and checked on 2026-10-31.

## Who can have one
- **Employers:** generally small businesses with 100 or fewer employees that have no other retirement plan.
- **Employees:** generally anyone who earned at least $5,000 in each of any two prior years and expects to earn at least $5,000 this year. Employers may use looser rules but not stricter ones.

## How contributions work
- **Employer, one of two options each year:** match employee contributions dollar for dollar up to 3% of pay (the employer may lower this to no less than 1%), or contribute 2% of each eligible employee's pay even if the employee contributes nothing.
- **Employee:** may choose salary-reduction contributions. For 2026 the IRS page lists a $17,000 limit, a $4,000 catch-up for employees 50 or older, and a higher $5,250 catch-up for ages 60 to 63. These amounts change by year.
- **Ownership:** employees are always 100% vested.

## Pros and cons from the page
Easy and inexpensive to set up and run, with no discrimination testing. Contributions are inflexible and the limits are lower than for some other plans. Loans are not permitted.

## Early withdrawals
Withdrawals are taxable. A 10% additional tax applies before age 59½, rising to 25% if the withdrawal is within the first two years of participating.
