# Treasury Bonds

A Treasury bond is a long-term debt security sold by the U.S. Department of the Treasury. This article is based on the TreasuryDirect page checked on 2026-10-31.

## Key facts
- **Term:** "either 20 or 30 years."
- **Interest:** fixed, paid every six months until maturity. The rate is set at auction and, according to TreasuryDirect, "is never less than 0.125%."
- **Buying:** the minimum is $100, in $100 steps. The maximum is $10 million for a non-competitive bid or 35% of the offering amount for a competitive bid. Bonds are issued in electronic form only.
- **Auctions:** four a year for original issues and eight a year for reopenings.
- **Holding or selling:** you can hold the bond until it matures or sell it earlier. Treasury bonds are eligible for STRIPS.

## Not the same as savings bonds
Treasury bonds are different from U.S. savings bonds (Series EE, I and HH).

## Taxes
Interest is subject to federal income tax each year. There are no state or local taxes.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
