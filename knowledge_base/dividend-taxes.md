# Dividends and How They Are Taxed

This article summarizes IRS Topic No. 404, last updated 2026-10-08, and checked on 2026-10-31. The page does not state tax rates or holding-period rules, so this article does not either; see IRS Publication 550 or the Form 1040 instructions for the tax year.

## What dividends are
"Dividends are distributions of earnings and profits paid by a corporation to stockholders." They are usually paid in cash but can also come as stock, stock rights or other property. Mutual funds, ETFs, money market funds and real estate investment trusts (REITs) also pay dividends that pass through to their shareholders.

## Ordinary vs. qualified dividends
All taxable dividends are ordinary income. **Qualified dividends** are the subset of ordinary dividends that can be taxed at the lower capital gain rates. The company paying the dividend identifies which ones are qualified on Form 1099-DIV.

## Other kinds of distributions
- **Capital gain distributions** from funds and REITs are always long-term capital gains.
- **Return of capital** is not a dividend. It reduces your cost basis in the stock. Once your basis reaches zero, further nondividend distributions are taxable capital gains.
- Dividends on deposits at mutual savings banks, credit unions and similar institutions are actually interest and are reported as interest income.

## Reporting
- **Form 1099-DIV** is sent by each payer for distributions of at least $10.
- **Schedule B (Form 1040)** is required if you receive more than $1,500 of taxable ordinary dividends.
- The net investment income tax may apply if you receive significant dividends.
