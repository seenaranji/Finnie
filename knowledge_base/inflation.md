# What Is Inflation?

This article is based on a Federal Reserve FAQ page last updated August 22, 2025, and checked on 2026-10-31.

## The idea
Inflation is a rise in prices across the economy over time. A price increase for one product, or even several, does not count. It is "a general increase in the overall price level" of goods and services.

## How it is measured
Fed policymakers watch several price indexes, because each one covers different goods and services and is calculated differently. The main one the Fed uses is the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), produced by the Department of Commerce, largely because it covers a wide range of household spending. The Fed also looks at the consumer price indexes and producer price indexes from the Department of Labor.

## How officials read it
- They average inflation over periods from a few months to a year or longer, because monthly figures can swing.
- They look at subcategories to judge whether a price rise is likely to be temporary or to last.
- They watch "core" measures, which usually leave out volatile food and energy prices, to see the underlying trend.

## Why it matters to investors
Rising prices reduce what a dollar can buy, so returns are often judged after inflation. See the articles on the Fed's 2 percent goal and on TIPS.
