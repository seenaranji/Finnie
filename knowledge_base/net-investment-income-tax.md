# The Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)

The net investment income tax (NIIT) is an extra 3.8% tax on certain investment income for people whose income is above set thresholds. This article summarizes IRS Topic No. 559, last updated 2026-09-24, and checked on 2026-10-31. Thresholds and figures can change, so check the current IRS page.

## What counts as net investment income
- Interest, dividends, certain annuities, royalties and rents
- Income from a passive business activity, or from trading in financial instruments or commodities
- Gains from selling property such as stocks, bonds, mutual funds and real estate
- Generally, gains from selling an interest in an active partnership or S corporation

Not included: wages, unemployment compensation and income from an active business. Certain income excluded from regular income tax, such as tax-exempt municipal bond interest, is also exempt.

## Who owes it
The tax is 3.8% of the lesser of your net investment income or the amount by which your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) is above your threshold. For individuals the IRS lists these thresholds:
- $250,000 for married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse
- $125,000 for married filing separately
- $200,000 for single or head of household

Estates and trusts have their own threshold, tied to where the highest tax bracket begins.

## Reporting
The tax is figured on Form 8960 and reported on Form 1040 (individuals) or Form 1041 (estates and trusts). Because the tax can add to what you owe, some taxpayers adjust their withholding or estimated tax payments.
