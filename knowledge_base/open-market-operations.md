# Open Market Operations

This article is based on a Federal Reserve page, checked on 2026-10-31.

## What they are
The Federal Reserve defines open market operations (OMOs) as "the purchase and sale of securities in the open market by a central bank." They are a key tool for carrying out monetary policy.

## Who does them
The Federal Reserve conducts them. The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) sets their short-term objective.

## What they have been used for, according to the page
- **Managing the federal funds rate:** before the global financial crisis, the Fed used OMOs to adjust the supply of reserve balances and keep the federal funds rate, "the interest rate at which depository institutions lend reserve balances to other depository institutions overnight," near the FOMC's target.
- **Supporting the economy after 2008:** from late 2008 through October 2014, the Fed bought large amounts of longer-term securities to push down longer-term interest rates and support economic activity.
- **Keeping reserves ample and rates steady:** starting in December 2015 the Fed used overnight reverse repurchase agreements to help control the federal funds rate, and starting in 2019 it used term and overnight repurchase agreements to keep reserves plentiful and ease pressure in money markets.
- **Limiting rate spikes:** since 2021 the Fed has used standing repo operations to cap upward pressure on rates.
