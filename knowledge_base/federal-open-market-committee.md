# The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC)

This article is based on the Federal Reserve's FOMC page, checked on 2026-10-31. It leaves out the names of members, because they change.

## What it is
The FOMC is the Federal Reserve body responsible for open market operations. At its meetings it reviews economic and financial conditions and "determines the appropriate stance of monetary policy," and it assesses risks to the long-run goals of price stability and sustainable economic growth.

## Who is on it
The FOMC has twelve voting members: the seven members of the Board of Governors, the president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and four of the other eleven Reserve Bank presidents, who rotate in one-year terms. The other Reserve Bank presidents attend meetings and take part in discussions but do not vote.

## How often it meets
The page says the FOMC holds "eight regularly scheduled meetings per year."

## The federal funds rate
The Fed describes the federal funds rate as "the interest rate at which depository institutions lend balances at the Federal Reserve" to other depository institutions overnight. The Fed uses its policy tools to influence the supply of and demand for those balances, which in turn affects this rate.
