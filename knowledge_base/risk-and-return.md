# Risk and Return

In investing, risk is the chance that you will get a different result than you expected, including losing some or all of the money you put in. Return is what you earn, or lose, from an investment.

## The basic tradeoff
Investments that offer the chance of higher returns usually come with a higher chance of loss. Investments that are steadier tend to offer lower returns. There is no free lunch: be wary of anything that promises high returns with no risk.

## Common types of risk
- **Market risk:** the whole market falls and drags many investments down.
- **Company risk:** one company does badly.
- **Interest rate risk:** rising interest rates tend to push bond prices down.
- **Inflation risk:** rising prices reduce what your money can buy, so even "safe" cash can lose purchasing power over time.
- **Credit risk:** a borrower fails to pay back what it owes.
- **Liquidity risk:** you cannot sell quickly without accepting a poor price.

## Volatility
Volatility describes how much an investment's price swings up and down. A volatile investment can gain a lot or lose a lot in a short time. It is a common way to measure risk, but it is not the only kind.

## Risk tolerance and time horizon
- **Risk tolerance:** how much ups and downs a person can handle emotionally.
- **Risk capacity:** how much loss a person could afford financially.
- **Time horizon:** how long until the money is needed. Longer horizons give more time to recover from downturns, though recovery is never guaranteed.

## Past performance
How an investment did before does not predict how it will do next.

This article is general education, not advice about what you personally should do.
